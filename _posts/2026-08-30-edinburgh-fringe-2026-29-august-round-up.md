**Saturday 29 August** brought the big comedy verdict. After a week of
shortlists, parallel prizes and competing bits of late-Fringe momentum, the
[Edinburgh Comedy Awards 2026 winners](https://www.comedy.co.uk/fringe/news/8834/edinburgh-comedy-awards-2026/)
were announced.

## The winners

**Chris Cantrill** won Best Comedy Show for **[Rewilding](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/29/chris-cantrill-rewilding-review-monkey-barrel-comedy-edinburgh)**,
his rural whodunnit about village life, middle age and a mysterious act of
tree vandalism. British Comedy Guide notes that this was Cantrill's fourth
nomination, following two nominations as half of The Delightful Sausage and
one previous solo nomination.

**Marty Gleeson** won Best Newcomer for **Dog Ear**, her art-school-inspired
show. It completes a strong awards week for Gleeson, who also won Best
Newcomer at the peer-voted Comedians' Choice Awards.

The Panel Prize went to **[One Joke](https://www.comedy.co.uk/fringe/fyi/1160/one-joke-lineup/)**,
the charity show that brought 60 comedians to Pleasance Dome and gave each of
them exactly one gag. It is hard to imagine a tidier Panel Prize choice in a
year when the best Fringe stories were often about form as much as content.

## Fourth time lucky

The Guardian's
[winners report](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/29/chris-cantrill-edinburgh-comedy-award-2026)
frames Cantrill's victory as a fourth-time-lucky moment. It also makes the
nice historical point that Amy Gledhill, Cantrill's comedy partner in The
Delightful Sausage, won the same prize in 2024. Both halves of the double act
have now won Edinburgh's top comedy award within three years.

Cantrill's win also says something about this year's shortlist. The nominees
were not clustered around one obvious theme. **Dan Tiernan**, **Harriet
Dyer**, **Lara Ricote**, **Li Jin Hao** and **Spencer Jones** all arrived
with very different arguments for the prize. In that field, a comic rural
mystery with a clear storytelling spine may have looked like the most
complete package.

## Reviews of the day

The Guardian's Saturday reviews gave the awards day a useful final layer:

- **[Chris Cantrill - *Rewilding*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/29/chris-cantrill-rewilding-review-monkey-barrel-comedy-edinburgh)**
  was reviewed as a playful, characterful village mystery that keeps finding
  laughs in awkward small talk, social embarrassment and the gap between
  wanting community and finding it hard to live inside one.

- **[Underground Monk Show](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/29/underground-monk-show-review-assembly-george-square-gardens-edinburgh)**
  received a late review for its surreal mixture of clowning, ritual, robes
  and mystical nonsense. It did not win Best Newcomer, but its nomination
  already confirms that this year's awards were willing to make space for very
  odd work.

- **[The Trials of Magnus Coffinkey](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/29/the-trials-of-magnus-coffinkey-review-summerhall-edinburgh)**
  sits outside the comedy-awards story, but its fairytale treatment of grief
  is a useful reminder that the wider festival does not stop just because the
  comedy prizes are handed out.

## The shape of the year

The final awards picture is satisfyingly untidy. The official Edinburgh
Comedy Awards went to **Chris Cantrill**, **Marty Gleeson** and **One Joke**.
ISH split Best Show between **Harriet Dyer** and **Paul Foot**, and named
**Sam Eley** Best Newcomer. The List picked **Conk** as Best Comedy Show. The
Comedians' Choice Awards chose **Kate Dolan** and **Marty Gleeson**.

That spread is useful. If one awards body had swept the month into a single
story, it would have made the Fringe look smaller than it was. Instead, the
2026 comedy conversation ends with a set of overlapping maps: official
recognition, peer approval, critic enthusiasm and word of mouth all pointing
in related but different directions.

## Today's takeaway

Awards day gives the festival a headline, but it does not close the argument.
Cantrill, Gleeson and One Joke are deserving winners; so are several acts
recognised elsewhere this week. The healthiest thing about Edinburgh comedy in
2026 is that no single list quite contains it.
