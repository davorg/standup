**Thursday 13 August** brought ambitious storytelling, inventive staging and a
Hamlet parody in which the ghost demanding vengeance is a slaughtered pig.
The Fringe has reached the point where that final detail barely needs
explaining.

Our team was back in London, but the shows, reviews and arguments continued in
Edinburgh.

## At the Fringe

The Guardian reviewed **Jordan Brookes – *The Part of You That's Always
Screaming***, finding the former Comedy Award winner in a more conventional
but still spiky and interrogative mode. Its other reviews today demonstrated
the festival's range:

- **Hannah Maxwell – *Turn Your F*cking Phones Off*** turns quitting
  doomscrolling into an attack on big tech and smartphone complicity.
- **Ham** recasts Shakespeare's tragedy as a riotous vegan revenge story about
  the meat industry.

All three can be found in the Guardian's
[Edinburgh comedy coverage](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/comedy).

Chortle's expanding
[review index](https://www.chortle.co.uk/about/2026/07/20/61020/edinburgh_fringe_2026_comedy_reviews)
also has Lara Ricote and Bebe Cave among its highest-rated shows, alongside
Frankie McNair, Celia Pacquola and Sam Eley's *Basil Crumbwick: Soul Sewage*.

## Today's takeaway

The strongest shows today were not afraid to make their audiences work. A
Fringe hour can be funny without being simple, and entertaining without
leaving every question neatly answered.
