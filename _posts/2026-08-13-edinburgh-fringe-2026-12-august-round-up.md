**Wednesday 12 August** was our final day of shows before the train back to
London. We finished with two ambitious pieces of comedy theatre: an
irreverent slice of Victorian art history and a supernatural story with some
genuine scares.

## Our final shows

- **Bebe Cave – *Swoon*** tells a cheerfully unreliable version of Lizzie
  Siddal's life through the eyes of a fictional sister. Historical liberties,
  increasingly elaborate hats and a formidable performance make it
  educational, entertaining and very funny.
  → [Read our 4½-star review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#bebe-cave-swoon)

- **Crybabies – *The Scaring*** tells the story of a former priest visiting a
  remote hotel with serious supernatural problems. Michael Clarke, James
  Gault and Ed Jones pack the hour with jokes while committing fully enough to
  the horror to deliver some genuine jump scares. The staging achieves effects
  far beyond what we expected from a Fringe comedy show.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#crybabies-the-scaring)

## Comedy and true crime

Amanda Knox's *Cartwheel* remains the festival's biggest story outside the
arts pages. [The Week's overview](https://theweek.com/culture-life/theatre/amanda-knox-meredith-kercher-real-crime-stand-up-show-at-edinburgh-fringe)
places it alongside other shows drawing on real crime and asks what happens
when traumatic events become stand-up material.

Critical opinion is divided. Several newspapers have responded warmly to
Knox's account, while
[Chortle's review](https://www.chortle.co.uk/review/2026/08/12/61275/amanda_knox%3A_cartwheel)
was considerably less persuaded by it as comedy. That disagreement is more
interesting than a quick consensus would have been.

## Today's takeaway

Our visit was short, but it covered stand-up, theatre, dance, a solo musical,
cardboard sets, a language seminar and Victorian history. The reporting will
continue from London as reviews, recommendations and awards contenders emerge.
