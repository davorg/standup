Every year, newspapers and websites devote acres of coverage to the Fringe's
biggest names. They're easy to write about because everyone already knows who
they are.

But the Edinburgh Fringe has always been about discovery. Somewhere in this
year's programme are comedians who will be headlining theatres, hosting TV
shows or collecting Comedy Awards in a few years' time.

None of us knows exactly who those people are yet. But these are eleven
performers whose names keep cropping up whenever comics, critics and Fringe
regulars start talking about the acts to watch in 2026.

### Bebe Cave

Bebe Cave is not arriving unnoticed: her debut hour *Christbride* won Best
Comedy Show at the 2025 List Festival Awards. She specialises in ambitious
character comedy that turns history, art and the treatment of women into
something very funny and pleasingly strange. Her new show,
[*Swoon*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/bebe-cave-swoon), moves
into Victorian England and the world of the pre-Raphaelites, looking at the
men behind the paintings and the women they preferred as beautiful, silent
muses. The subject gives Cave plenty of room for theatrical invention as well
as a sharp attack on male artistic self-importance. She already has an award;
this could be the year a much wider audience discovers why.

### Kate Dolan

Kate Dolan's comedy is rapid, restless and gloriously off-centre: jokes,
voices, physical flourishes and sudden asides all competing for space. That
energy has already travelled well. She won both the Skinny-Fest Festival
Breakthrough Award and the Melbourne International Comedy Festival's Golden
Gibbo for independent comedy in 2025. Her new hour,
[*Trout*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/kate-dolan-trout), starts
with the wonderfully odd question of whether life as a fish in a river might
be preferable to life as a woman on land. Dolan has been building momentum in
more than one comedy scene; Edinburgh now has the chance to turn that growing
reputation into a proper UK breakthrough.

### Rowan Hackett

Scottish comic Rowan Hackett takes a confessional subject and gives it an
unusual tactical formation. [*Brewed*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/rowan-hackett-brewed)
examines her complicated relationship with alcohol, reality and family
through the language of football. It follows her from the vodka-and-lemonade
years to IPA, parenthood and pizza-crunch suppers, combining self-deprecation
with densely packed one-liners. The football conceit sounds like more than a
decorative theme: it gives Hackett a way to make a personal story playful and
specific without sanding down its difficult edges. Home-grown acts can be
easy to overlook amid the thousands of visiting performers; Hackett looks
like a strong reason not to make that mistake this year.

### Sharon Wanjohi

Sharon Wanjohi already has the kind of momentum that often precedes a bigger
Fringe moment. She was a Chortle Best Newcomer nominee in 2025, has appeared
on the *Off Menu* podcast and has supported comics including Sarah Keyworth
and Jessica Fostekew. [*A Perfect Life*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/sharon-wanjohi-a-perfect-life)
imagines stripping life of risk, conflict, embarrassing phases and all the
other things that might cause emotional turbulence. Inevitably, the safer the
plan becomes, the less living it seems to contain. It is a fertile premise for
Wanjohi's high-energy style: a show about fear and control that also questions
the modern urge to optimise every last trace of mess out of ourselves.

### Daniel Petrie

Daniel Petrie has an unusually convincing double life: he is both an NHS
doctor and an award-winning Scottish surrealist stand-up. He won Leicester
Mercury Comedian of the Year in 2023, then reached the finals of both the
Chortle Student Comedy Awards and the BBC New Comedy Award in 2025. At this
Fringe he shares [a bill with Valeria Vulpe](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/daniel-petrie-and-valeria-vulpe),
a Moldovan mathematician turned deadpan comic who recently opened for Ricky
Gervais at Wembley Arena after winning the 2025 Dutch Barn Spirit of Comedy.
That makes the hour a particularly good scouting opportunity: two distinctive
new acts in one show, with Petrie's surreal viewpoint and increasingly strong
competition record suggesting that his half could be especially worth watching.

### Alex Kitson

Alex Kitson arrives with two acclaimed Fringe hours behind him and an enviable
pile of five-star reviews. His 2025 show was among the best-reviewed comedy at
the festival and earned him an ISH Edinburgh Comedy Awards Best Newcomer
nomination, as well as a place on the longlist for Dave's Funniest Joke of the
Fringe. [*Now Look Here*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/alex-kitson-now-look-here)
is a new hour about attention and the increasingly novel pleasure of doing
things in real life. That is a broad subject, but Kitson's reputation rests on
turning close observation into carefully constructed, original routines. If
the reviews convert into word of mouth and full rooms, 2026 may be when the
audience catches up with the critics.

### Hasan Al-Habib

Hasan Al-Habib's 2025 run was extended after selling out, put him on both *The
Times* and *Telegraph* lists of the Fringe's funniest jokes and brought a
NextUp Biggest Award in Comedy nomination. His new solo hour,
[*Stuck in the Middle (East) With You*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/hasan-al-habib-stuck-in-the-middle-east-with-you),
began with an agent's request for material more relatable to white British
audiences; Al-Habib's answer is a show about growing up as a child of divorce.
He is also appearing with Aisha Amanduri in the gameshow-inflected
[*2 Muslim 2 Furious 3: Sharia? I Hardly Know Her!*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/2-muslim-2-furious-3-sharia-i-hardly-know-her),
with ten per cent of proceeds going to Medical Aid for Palestinians. Two very
different formats should show just how versatile he is.

### Jin Hao Li

Li Jin Hao's debut, *Swimming in a Submarine*, was nominated for the Edinburgh
Comedy Award for Best Newcomer in 2024. His dry, dreamlike stand-up slips
between precise observations and images that seem to have floated in from a
different reality, giving him a stage voice that is already unmistakably his
own. [*Falling From a Moon*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/li-jin-hao-falling-from-a-moon)
begins with an addiction to imaginary time travel and a desire to revisit a
past that felt lighter than the present—not so far back as Pangaea, but perhaps
as far as the Moon. Li is beyond the completely unknown stage; the question
this year is whether his singular comic world can carry him from admired cult
discovery to a substantially larger audience.

### Emma Doran

Emma Doran is already a major name in Irish comedy, but 2026 marks her
Edinburgh Fringe debut and an opportunity to reach a much larger audience in
Britain. She is a best-selling author and co-hosts the *Keep It Tight* podcast
with Deirdre O'Kane, while her television appearances include *The Tommy
Tiernan Show*, *The Late Late Show* and Prime Video's *LOL: Last One
Laughing*. [*Emmaculate*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/emma-doran-emmaculate)
draws on the trials, alliances and complications of her blended family,
combining sharp observation with the conspiratorial pleasure of being told
excellent gossip. Doran may not be a discovery to audiences at home, but a
successful first Edinburgh run could be the step that makes her equally well
known across the UK.

### Crybabies

Crybabies are the least secret discovery on this list: the sketch trio have
already been nominated for the Edinburgh Comedy Award and won formidable
reviews for their densely written, theatrical storytelling. Their main 2026
show, [*The Scaring*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/crybabies-the-scaring),
sends a former priest to a remote hotel for a horror-comedy about fear, faith,
fathers and phantoms, directed by Jon Brittain. They are also offering
[*Eye Infections*](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/crybabies-eye-infections),
a stripped-back collection of standalone sketches for anyone who likes the
group's writing but could do without a plot. Having both a large narrative
show and a deliberately lean companion piece makes this feel like an ideal
year to find out what the fuss is about.

### Ciara O'Connor

Ciara O'Connor has been accumulating exactly the kind of early credits that
make a newcomer worth following. She was a 2024 LGBTQ New Comedian of the Year
finalist, appeared in the North heat of the 2025 BBC New Comedy Awards and
reached the 2026 Leicester Comedian of the Year final. Her writing has also
featured on *RuPaul's Drag Race UK*, while her stand-up tackles important
questions such as why we dedicate benches to dead people. This year she is one
of four acts in [The Pleasance Comedy Reserve](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets/whats-on/the-pleasance-comedy-reserve),
the long-running showcase whose former members include Joe Lycett, Roisin
Conaty, Sophie Duker and Jack Whitehall. There could hardly be a clearer
invitation to see her before she graduates to a solo hour.
