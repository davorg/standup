**Tuesday 11 August** took us from dance-theatre and the stranger corners of
language to heartfelt stand-up and an intricately constructed show about
communication. National comedy coverage was gathering pace too.

## Our discoveries

- **Vibhinna Ramdev – *Why English?*** combines dance, theatre and personal
  storytelling with an examination of India's history and the position of
  women in traditional families. Ramdev is a compelling performer and the
  result is bold, thoughtful and deeply personal.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#why-english)

- **Joe Mayo – *The Ship of Thesaurus (WIP)*** explores collective nouns,
  signal flags and the stranger corners of Wikipedia in the smallest room we
  visited this year. At times it feels like a particularly entertaining group
  discussion.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#the-ship-of-thesaurus-wip)

- **Jules Oakes – *Fragile*** is advertised by its performer walking around
  Edinburgh dressed as a pigeon. Beneath that excellent sales technique is a
  warm show about neurodivergence and deciding to stop hiding your real self.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#jules-oakes-fragile)

- **Lara Ricote – *INKLING*** is an intricately structured exploration of
  communication, uncertainty and Ricote's experience of being hard of
  hearing. Its deliberate complexity sometimes creates distance, but the
  intelligence and care behind it are undeniable.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#lara-ricote-inkling)

## The critics' day

The Guardian gave enthusiastic coverage to **Frankie McNair's *Huge Ass
Mindset***, a show that finds joy, defiance and very dark laughs in traumatic
experience. Its comedy coverage also considered **Amanda Knox's *Cartwheel***,
judging the inexperienced stand-up's act of reclaiming her story more
successful than many expected.

Those reviews sit alongside pieces on Harriet Dyer and the romantic comedy
*Spin Cycle* on the Guardian's
[comedy page](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/comedy).
For a broader snapshot, Chortle's
[Fringe review index](https://www.chortle.co.uk/about/2026/07/20/61020/edinburgh_fringe_2026_comedy_reviews)
now ranges from five-star recommendations to some useful warnings.

## Today's takeaway

Comedy does not have to mean a person, a microphone and a sequence of jokes.
Today it meant dance, colonial history, signal flags, a pigeon costume and an
ambitious attempt to make an audience experience communication differently.
