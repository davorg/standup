**Tuesday 25 August** was the eve of the big comedy shortlists. The official
Edinburgh Comedy Awards and the ISH Edinburgh Comedy Awards are both due to
announce their shortlists on Wednesday, so today's reviews and award news felt
like the last breath before the final-week rush becomes formal.

## Reviews of the day

The Guardian's 25 August coverage moved between personal stand-up, workplace
farce and a feature on fatherhood:

- **[Kemah Bob - *Love Child*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/25/kemah-bob-love-child-review-pleasance-courtyard-edinburgh)**
  gives Bob a more reflective follow-up to *Miss Fortunate*. The review finds
  a playful origin story built from family history, southern Black childhood,
  inherited trauma and the awkward business of trying to become an adult
  while working in comedy.

- **[Kate Cheka - *The Last Funny Woman*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/25/kate-cheka-the-last-funny-woman-review)**
  finds Cheka eight months pregnant and still full of force. The show sounds
  like a sharp mix of romantic storytelling, political frustration and a
  serious challenge to a comedy industry that still struggles to support
  marginalised voices properly.

- **[The Librarians - *A Very Serious Comedy!*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/25/the-librarians-a-very-serious-comedy-review-pleasance-courtyard-edinburgh)**
  is a workplace comedy set in a failing library. The review hears echoes of
  classic British sitcoms and finds a familiar but frequently funny hour,
  helped along by well-tuned performances and physical comedy.

The same day's
[feature on fatherhood at the Fringe](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/25/well-balanced-dads-lions-olaf-falafel-helium-balloon-factory-edinburgh-fringe-stage)
looked at **Well-Balanced Dads**, **Lions** and **Olaf Falafel's *I Used to
Work in a Helium Balloon Factory Until They Let Me Go***. It is a useful
late-festival theme: parenthood treated not as tidy sentiment, but as a place
where embarrassment, grief, identity and absurdity all collide.

## The List shortlist

The List Festival Awards have now
[announced their 2026 shortlists](https://list.co.uk/news/shortlists-revealed-for-the-list-festival-awards-2026-48985).
In the Fringe comedy category, the final three are:

- **Alfie Brown - *The Entertainer***
- **Conk - *Man Sings The Same Song Over And Over Again For An Hour***
- **Elf Lyons - *The Woman On The Edge***

That is a pleasingly varied trio: stand-up about crisis and meaning, an
absurd endurance stunt that has become one of the year's word-of-mouth hits,
and Lyons' Vertigo-inspired autobiographical hour. The winners will be
announced on **Friday 28 August**.

The full shortlist also covers cabaret, dance, circus, kids' shows, LGBTQIA+
work and shows from Adelaide. That breadth matters because late-Fringe award
coverage can otherwise make the festival look narrower than it is.

## The awards bottleneck

The next 48 hours will be crowded. The Comedians' Choice Awards winners are
due on Wednesday afternoon. The Edinburgh Comedy Awards and ISH shortlists are
also expected on Wednesday, with the ISH winners on Friday and the Edinburgh
Comedy Awards ceremony on Saturday.

That sequence is good for focus, but it can also distort attention. Once a
shortlist appears, ticket demand and press interest concentrate very quickly.
The useful thing to remember is that every shortlist is a selection, not a
settlement.

## Today's takeaway

On 25 August, the Fringe still looked wide open: polished stand-up, old-school
farce, pregnancy, parenthood, queer friendship, endurance comedy and several
different awards systems all competing to define the final week. That is a
healthier picture than any single prize can capture.
