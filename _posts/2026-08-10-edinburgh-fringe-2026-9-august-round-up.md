**Sunday 9 August** was arrival day for the StandUp.co.uk team. One of us was
returning to the Fringe after 25 years; the other was seeing it for the first
time. Edinburgh did not offer either of us a gentle introduction.

## First impressions

Our accommodation made a memorable first impression. The entrance looked
more like the start of a particularly grim piece of immersive theatre than
the way into our home for the next few days. Happily, it was much nicer once
we got through the door.

![The graffiti-covered entrance to our Edinburgh accommodation](/assets/images/edinburgh-accommodation.jpg)

*Not the most reassuring entrance—but it was much nicer inside.*

## From spreadsheets to streets

The programme may be searchable, sortable and carefully added to calendars,
but it feels very different when every lamppost carries six posters and every
few metres brings another flyer. The official numbers—3,649 shows and more
than 53,000 performances—stop being statistics and start looking like a
personal challenge.

They are not. The most important thing we learned today was to accept that we
will miss almost everything.

## Our first shows

- **Olga Koch – *Fat Tom Cruise*** weaves a fascinating and entertaining
  story whose destination is not immediately obvious. The disorientation is
  part of the pleasure: when everything comes together, it is clear that Koch
  has been in control throughout.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#olga-koch-fat-tom-cruise)

- **Ele McKenzie – *Bringing It All Back Home*** has interesting—and very
  rude—things to say about adults moving back in with their parents. The Bob
  Dylan-inspired poster did its job and drew us in.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#ele-mckenzie-bringing-it-all-back-home)

## What people are recommending

Opening-weekend audiences have been particularly enthusiastic about physical
and alternative comedy. Elf Lyons, Piotr Sikora's *Blizzard*, Viggo Venn and
*Mr Cardboard* have all appeared in early recommendations. Sophie Duker, Kemah
Bob, Tom Cashman and Madeline Brettingham are also generating warm word of
mouth. The
[opening-weekend discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/edinburghfringe/comments/1vk56ps/the_best_comedy_shows_from_opening_weekend/)
is worth browsing for tips, with the usual warning that one stranger's
masterpiece is another stranger's baffling hour in a basement.

The Guardian's
[essential comedy selection](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/jun/30/funny-comedy-edinburgh-fringe-2026)
provides a more curated counterweight, while the official
[ticket search](https://www.edfringe.com/tickets) is the place to check current
times and availability.

That is the pleasure of the Fringe: everybody attends the same event, but no
two people experience the same festival. Ours has now started with two very
different hours of comedy.
