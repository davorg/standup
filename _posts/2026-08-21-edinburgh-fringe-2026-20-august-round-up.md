**Thursday 20 August** was one of those Fringe days when comedy seemed to
scatter in every direction at once: horror theatre, improvised musicals,
smutty nostalgia, character work and the annual argument about one-liners.

## Reviews of the day

The Guardian gave
**[Crybabies' *The Scaring*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/20/crybabies-the-scaring-review-pleasance-courtyard-edinburgh)**
another strong notice, praising the Radio 4 trio's blend of horror plotting,
theatrical illusion and rapid jokes. That is useful confirmation for anyone
who caught the show earlier in the run and wondered whether the jump scares
were carrying as well from the back row as they were in the room.

The same page also covered
**[Making Love with David Magidoff](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/20/making-love-with-david-magidoff-review-fran-healy-assembly-george-square-edinburgh)**,
an improvised musical built from a guest's romantic history. This performance
used Fran Healy's teenage infidelity as its raw material. The result sounds
loose and occasionally incoherent, but still very Fringe: a one-night premise,
a guest with a story and a room willing to see what can be made of it.

## Joke of the Fringe

British Comedy Guide reported that
[Olaf Falafel has won the 2026 Dave Joke of the Fringe](https://www.comedy.co.uk/fringe/news/8817/olaf-falafel-wins-fringe-of-the-fringe-2026/).
His winning line involved a piranha, a boomerang and the inevitable
consequence of combining the two.

Falafel also appeared elsewhere in the top ten, which will not surprise
anyone who has followed the joke competitions over the last decade. These
lists are always a strange mix: part publicity exercise, part genuine craft
test, part annual invitation for comedians to complain about what a joke is.
Still, a clean one-liner can cut through the noise of a festival with more
than four thousand shows.

## Character, costume and filth

The Guardian's
[feature on strange comic creations](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/20/how-four-edinburgh-stars-created-their-weird-characters)
gathered performers who have built entire shows around extreme personas and
visual transformations. Another
[piece looked at the return of smut](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/20/julian-clary-fully-dilated-smut-edinburgh-fringe-comedy-entendre),
with Julian Clary's *Fully Dilated* placed alongside newer performers using
explicit comedy for confession, escape and control.

The useful thing about both features is that they take silliness seriously.
At the Fringe, a costume, a pun or a filthy double entendre can be a cheap
laugh, but it can also be the door into something more personal.

## Today's takeaway

The 20 August snapshot is gloriously untidy. Crybabies are making comedy from
possession-horror mechanics, Magidoff is improvising around celebrity
confession, Olaf Falafel is winning with an engineered groaner, and the
festival is still asking how much seriousness can hide inside nonsense.
