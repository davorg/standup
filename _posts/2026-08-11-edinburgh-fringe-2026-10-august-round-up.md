**Monday 10 August** was our first full day of shows. It provided a neat
cross-section of the Fringe: a personal science-fiction musical, several
contrasting stand-up sets, a woman dressed as a trout and two performers
staging the end of the world with more enthusiasm than scenery.

## From our notebook

- **Midge Lema – *Bean vs The Robots*** uses science fiction as the framework
  for a much more personal story about loss and rebuilding a life. We found it
  imaginative and heartfelt, even if the songs could have used more variety.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#bean-vs-the-robots-a-solo-show-musical)

- **Tom Johnson and Rob Glenister – *Armageddon Outta Here*** represent the
  chaotic, cardboard-set side of the Fringe at its most enjoyable. An asteroid
  is approaching Earth, but the plot mainly exists to support puns, sight gags
  and slapstick.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#armageddon-outta-here)

- **Lilla Multipass – *Woman(33)*** takes the audience into a first therapy
  session and deconstructs the life of a millennial woman using stand-up,
  theatre, cardboard boxes and remarkably gentle audience participation. It
  is a joy—and, if there is any justice, an awards contender.
  → [Read our 4½-star review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#lilla-multipass-woman-33)

- **Daniel Petrie and Valeria Vulpe** offer two contrasting sets: great jokes
  about ordinary life from Petrie, then Vulpe's bone-dry humour and an
  unexpected quantity of information about Moldova.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#daniel-petrie-and-valeria-vulpe)

- **Kate Dolan – *Trout*** begins with an elaborate fish head and a warning
  that the show will not get any more normal. Loud, physical and
  confrontational, it is strange, exhausting and often very funny.
  → [Read our review](/reviews/2026/edinburgh/#kate-dolan-trout)

## Reviews begin to arrive

The national papers have started publishing from Edinburgh. The Guardian
reviewed Harriet Richardson's *Creep*—a performance about shame and desire—and
*Titclown: Daddy's Little Girl*, described as a smart response to the mess
made by men. Its regularly updated
[comedy page](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/comedy)
is an easy way to follow the coverage.

Meanwhile, Chortle's
[review index](https://www.chortle.co.uk/about/2026/07/20/61020/edinburgh_fringe_2026_comedy_reviews)
is already filling with everything from conventional stand-up to shows called
*Man Sings the Same Song Over and Over Again for an Hour*.

## Today's takeaway

A Fringe day improves when its shows have almost nothing in common. Robots,
an asteroid, therapy, Moldova and a trout will do nicely.
