**Sunday 16 August** brought the Fringe's second weekend to a close. With our
team back in London, we widened the net: national reviews, specialist comedy
coverage, industry news and the recommendations audiences are passing between
themselves.

## Reviews of the day

The Guardian published three very different Edinburgh reviews:

- **Abby Govindan – *Pushing 30*** is a confident debut using pranks,
  PowerPoint and easygoing storytelling to challenge patriarchal assumptions
  about marriage. Govindan treats marriage as a possible side quest rather
  than the compulsory destination of a woman's twenties.

- **Jonno – *Here Comes Mr Funny*** gives Daran Johnson—head writer of
  *Saturday Night Live UK* and one-third of sketch group Sheeps—his first solo
  hour. The review praises a performance that balances sincerity and shtick
  while delivering some very big laughs.

- **The Last Drop**, part of the Fringe's São Paulo Showcase, is an
  atmospheric dark domestic comedy by Dante Passarelli. It borrows from
  *Hamlet* to explore a household in which, as the review puts it, everything
  is rotten.

All three are linked from the Guardian's regularly updated
[comedy coverage](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/comedy).

## The specialist verdict so far

Chortle's
[2026 Fringe review index](https://www.chortle.co.uk/about/2026/07/20/61020/edinburgh_fringe_2026_comedy_reviews)
has now grown into a useful snapshot of the festival. Its five-star group
contains **Sam Eley's *Basil Crumbwick: Soul Sewage***, **Celia Pacquola's
*Gift Horse*** and **Frankie McNair's *Huge Ass Mindset***—as well as our own
favourite **Bebe Cave's *Swoon***.

The 4½-star group is also striking. It includes **Andrew White – *What a
Life!*** and **Kate Dehnert – *Echo*** alongside two shows we saw and strongly
recommended: **Lara Ricote's *INKLING*** and **Crybabies' *The Scaring***.

No ratings table can tell you whether a show will suit your taste, but the
index is now broad enough to be valuable when filling a gap in a schedule.

## What audiences are recommending

One of today's most useful audience posts contains
[dozens of concise mini-reviews](https://www.reddit.com/r/edinburghfringe/comments/1vpyyfy/edfringe_minireviews/)
from across the programme. Its five-star recommendations demonstrate why it
pays to look beyond stand-up:

- *American Idiot*, performed by a young Scottish musical-theatre cast;
- *A Wolf Shall Devour the Sun*, an evening of imaginative storytelling;
- *When Vincent Met John*, imagining a meeting between Van Gogh and Lennon;
- *CODE*, combining parkour and trials-bike stunts with a story about young
  people being drawn into crime;
- *Fly You Fools*, a three-person, shot-by-shot sprint through *The Lord of
  the Rings*; and
- *YOAH*, contemporary Japanese circus and acrobatics.

That is six recommendations covering musicals, storytelling, theatre,
physical performance, comedy and circus. It is difficult to imagine a better
advertisement for an unplanned Fringe afternoon.

## Behind the festival

The machinery surrounding the shows continues to evolve too. Among the
industry stories gathered on
[The Stage's Edinburgh Fringe page](https://www.thestage.co.uk/edinburgh-fringe)
are the launch of a theatre-discovery platform described as “Letterboxd for
theatre”, a trial of ticket lotteries for sold-out shows and concern about
travel disruption following a fire.

None of those stories is as glamorous as a five-star review, but discovery,
availability and simply getting into Edinburgh determine which shows find an
audience. At a festival of this scale, improving those systems matters.

## Today's takeaway

The critical conversation is becoming clearer, but the most interesting route
through the Fringe still mixes sources. Read the professional reviews, check
the specialist sites, then leave room for the parkour show or Tolkien parody
recommended by somebody who saw it yesterday.
