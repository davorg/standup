**Saturday 22 August** shifted attention back towards Scottish comedy. The
new McTavish Award announced its longlist, while the day's reviews continued
to show how broad the comedy-theatre boundary has become.

## The McTavish longlist

British Comedy Guide reported the
[first McTavish Award longlist](https://www.comedy.co.uk/fringe/fyi/1159/mctavish-award-2026-longlist/).
The prize recognises the outstanding Fringe show by a Scottish or
Scottish-based act and is named in memory of Paul Sneddon, better known as
Vladimir McTavish.

The ten longlisted acts are:

- **Amanda Dwyer - *Better Than Revenge***
- **Amy Matthews - *Definitions of Toast***
- **Chris Forbes - *Father Christmas***
- **Gareth Mutch - *Gareth Mutch Means Well***
- **Gareth Waugh - *Is Pepsi OK?***
- **Kim Blythe - *Puzzle***
- **Larry Dean - *Hellbent***
- **Paul McDaniel - *Harder Better Faster Paul-er***
- **Robin Grainger - *Lemonade***
- **Susie McCabe - *Coming of Rage***

The winner will be announced at The List Festival Awards on **Friday 28
August**. The prize package includes money, a run or tour with The Stand and
development mentoring with The Comedy Unit, which makes it a practical award
rather than just a certificate and a photograph.

## Reviews of the day

The Guardian reviewed
**[Elouise Eftos - *Aphrodite*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/22/elouise-eftos-aphrodite-review)**,
in which the performer continues playing with the persona of "Australia's
first attractive comedian". The same day's coverage included
**[Courtney Büchner - *One of the Girls*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/22/courtney-buchner-one-of-the-girls-review-pleasance-edinburgh)**,
a relationship show framed through fairground-ride momentum, and
**[Dirty Dancer](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/22/dirty-dancer-review-melissa-firlit)**,
a physical, fan-driven replay of an 80s favourite.

That is a useful trio: stand-up persona, personal storytelling and pop-culture
obsession all sitting close together under the comedy banner.

## Why local recognition matters

The McTavish Award arrives at a time when the Fringe's international scale can
make local work feel oddly easy to miss. Scottish acts do not need a separate
conversation because they are fragile; they need one because the festival is
so large that even strong work can disappear inside it.

The longlist also shows range within the Scottish scene: established names,
returning favourites and acts still building wider recognition.

## Today's takeaway

Awards are imperfect filters, but a well-designed one can change where people
look. Today, the useful instruction was simple: do not let the global Fringe
make you forget the comedy happening closest to home.
