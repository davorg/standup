**Friday 7 August** was the first official day of the 2026 Edinburgh Festival
Fringe. Previews had already been running for several days, but this was when
the festival properly declared itself open—and the scale of it became
inescapable.

## The Fringe opens

This year's programme contains **3,649 shows at 258 venues**, with artists from
71 countries giving 53,884 performances before the festival ends on 31 August.
The [official programme announcement](https://www.edfringe.com/about-us/news-and-blog/mixitup-with-the-2026-edinburgh-festival-fringe-programme/)
is a useful reminder that comedy is only one part of a festival that also
includes theatre, dance, circus, music, cabaret, children's shows and almost
anything else that can be performed in a room.

Artists also gained a permanent base this year. The new
[Fringe Central](https://www.edfringe.com/about-us/news-and-blog/first-look-of-new-fringe-central/)
at 6 Infirmary Street opened to participants today, offering support,
professional-development events and somewhere to pause amid the chaos.

## The first big argument

The festival's most widely reported comedy story was already Amanda Knox's
*Cartwheel*. Knox began an eleven-night run at Gilded Balloon after defending
the show against criticism from the family of Meredith Kercher. Knox said it
honoured Kercher's memory while allowing her to reclaim the story of her own
wrongful conviction. The
[Associated Press report](https://apnews.com/article/2f3976a72c2b77b16bd27dff6f7fa863)
sets out the dispute.

It is an uncomfortable story with no shortage of strongly held opinions. It
also raises an enduring Fringe question: who gets to turn painful experience
into a performance, and on what terms? Reviews over the coming days will tell
us whether *Cartwheel* works as comedy as well as testimony.

## A first-day tip

The official listings showed more than 2,000 performances today. Nobody can
make sense of that by studying the programme alone. Our approach for the month
remains simple: book the things we cannot bear to miss, leave room for word of
mouth and take chances on unfamiliar names.

The Fringe is now under way. Tomorrow the reviews and recommendations begin to
matter more than the previews.
