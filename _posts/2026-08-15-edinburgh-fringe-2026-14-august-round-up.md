**Friday 14 August** completed the Fringe's first full week. Enough reviews now
exist for audiences to arrive with expectations—and for performers to start
making the reviewers themselves part of the show.

## Critics under review

Two productions this year portray critics as dreadful snobs. In
[his Guardian column](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/14/a-critical-mauling-brian-logan-comedy-critics-reviewers-welcome-to-die),
Brian Logan submits to both attacks. Andrew Doherty's *Reviewers Welcome… To
Die!* turns the relationship into a murder mystery; another show places its
critic in a more straightforwardly hostile role.

There is an obvious risk in reviewing a show about reviewers: every criticism
can look like wounded professional pride. But the underlying argument is worth
having. A Fringe review can bring an unknown performer an audience, while a
careless dismissal can damage a show that has taken years and a frightening
amount of money to make.

## Reviews of the day

- **Kristen Schaal – *The Legend of Crystal Shell*** finds the *Flight of the
  Conchords* star playing a half-horse, half-woman who dreams of becoming a
  unicorn and performing *Swan Lake*. The Guardian found more sparkle than
  substance, but Schaal remains unlike anybody else at the festival.

- **Joseph Morpurgo – *Highlander 70*** builds an inventive show around a
  dork's final day at school. It also appears among Chortle's four-star
  recommendations.

- **Dear Michelle Kwan** uses figure skating as the basis for a whirlwind
  comedy about ambition and identity.

The reviews are collected on the Guardian's
[comedy page](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/comedy), while Chortle's
[Fringe index](https://www.chortle.co.uk/about/2026/07/20/61020/edinburgh_fringe_2026_comedy_reviews)
offers a much longer roll-call.

## End-of-week advice

Stars and reviews are filters, not instructions. Read enough to identify a
show that sounds like your kind of thing, then stop before every surprise has
been explained. Comedy still works best when you do not know exactly what is
coming.
