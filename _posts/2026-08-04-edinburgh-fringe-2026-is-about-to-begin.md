The waiting is almost over. The 2026 Edinburgh Festival Fringe begins on
Friday 7 August, when Edinburgh will once again become the most exciting,
exhausting and gloriously unpredictable place in the performing arts.

The StandUp.co.uk team arrives on Sunday 9 August. One of us is returning to
the Fringe after 25 years away; the other has never been before. Between us,
then, we should offer an interesting mixture of nostalgia, surprise and
complete disorientation.

## A festival on an extraordinary scale

The [official programme](https://www.edfringe.com/about-us/news-and-blog/mixitup-with-the-2026-edinburgh-festival-fringe-programme/)
contains **3,649 shows at 258 venues**, representing artists from **71
countries**. Those shows add up to **53,884 performances** between 7 and 31
August. There are 1,748 premieres, while 691 shows offer Pay What You Want or
Pay What You Can tickets.

No one can see more than a tiny fraction of that. This is not a problem to
solve; it is the point of the Fringe. Everyone leaves Edinburgh having
attended a different festival.

that "sold out" does not always mean "give up".

## How to get the most out of the Fringe

1. **Book the shows you would be genuinely upset to miss.** Popular acts and
   short runs can sell out, so secure the non-negotiable tickets first.

2. **Do not book every minute.** Leave gaps for recommendations, late meals,
   overrunning conversations and shows you did not know existed that morning.
   A rigid timetable can remove much of the fun.

3. **Plan by geography as well as time.** Edinburgh is hilly, the streets are
   crowded and venues with similar names may be nowhere near each other.
   Check the exact venue and room, then allow more walking time than the map
   suggests.

4. **Arrive early.** Many Fringe venues cannot admit latecomers, and finding
   the correct room inside a large venue can take longer than expected.

5. **Prepare for several seasons in one day.** Comfortable shoes, layers and
   something waterproof are more useful than an optimistic weather forecast.
   A charged phone and a small power bank are also worth carrying.

6. **Eat, drink water and occasionally stop.** Four or five consecutive shows
   can turn into an endurance event. A proper break will improve the rest of
   the day considerably.

7. **Take at least one chance.** Try a performer you have never heard of, a
   different genre or a Pay What You Want show. If you enjoy it, pay what you
   reasonably can: that money matters to the artist.

8. **Check access information before setting off.** The official listings
   include venue accessibility details and enhanced performances. Edinburgh's
   old buildings and temporary spaces vary enormously.

9. **Talk to people.** Ask audiences what they have loved, read the flyers and
   listen to performers recommending other performers. Word of mouth remains
   the Fringe's best discovery system.

## Comedy Awards dates for your diary

The [Edinburgh Comedy Awards](https://www.comedyawards.co.uk/about) will be one
of the major stories of the final week. The **Best Comedy Show and Best
Newcomer shortlists will be announced on Wednesday 26 August**, with the
**winners announced on Saturday 29 August**. The Panel Prize will be announced
alongside them.

We will be following the contenders as they emerge and publishing both the
shortlists and the results. Awards are not the only measure of a successful
Fringe—some of the shows people remember most never appear on a shortlist—but
they remain a fascinating snapshot of the year's comedy.
