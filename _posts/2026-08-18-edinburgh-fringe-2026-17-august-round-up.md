**Monday 17 August** began the Fringe's final fortnight with a useful reminder
that the shows people talk about are not always the tidy ones. The critical
conversation is now broad enough to reward both polished stand-up and ideas
that sound almost impossible to recommend in a single sentence.

## Reviews of the day

The Guardian's comedy coverage highlighted two shows that could hardly be
more different.

- **[Lara Ricote - *INKLING*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/17/lara-ricote-inkling-review)**
  continues to look like one of the festival's most intricate stand-up hours.
  The show uses Ricote's experience of hearing loss as the starting point for
  a deliberately complicated piece about communication, uncertainty and the
  limits of shared understanding.

- **[Conk - *Man Sings the Same Song Over and Over Again for an Hour*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/17/man-sings-the-same-song-over-and-over-again-for-an-hour-review)**
  is exactly as advertised. Connor Dariol repeats one song for a full hour,
  but the review argues that the discipline, tiny variations and commitment
  turn the stunt into something unexpectedly communal.

Chortle's
[Fringe review index](https://www.chortle.co.uk/about/2026/07/20/61020/edinburgh_fringe_2026_comedy_reviews)
now places both Ricote and Conk among its strongest-reviewed shows.

## The Fringe at its fringiest

Conk's show also appeared in the
[Financial Times' survey of the year's most "Fringey" experiences](https://www.ft.com/content/b81b5960-0340-4210-887f-47702360f907),
alongside lie-detector theatre, Darkfield's immersive installations, a sauna
version of *The Waves* and a late-night walking meditation. The point is not
that every audience member wants the strangest thing available. It is that
the festival still has room for work whose appeal depends on someone saying,
"No, honestly, it works."

That matters in a year when so many artists are competing for attention. A
straightforward four-star review can sell a show, but a premise that sounds
like a dare can travel by word of mouth even faster.

## Watching the awards shape up

The best-reviewed comedy shows are beginning to cluster. Chortle's
[current review index](https://www.chortle.co.uk/about/2026/07/20/61020/edinburgh_fringe_2026_comedy_reviews)
has a five-star group including **Bebe Cave's *Swoon***, **Celia Pacquola's
*Gift Horse***, **Frankie McNair's *Huge Ass Mindset***, **Sam Eley's *Basil
Crumbwick: Soul Sewage*** and **Sean Morley's *Backchannel***. The 4½-star
group includes **Andrew White**, **Crybabies**, **Kate Dehnert**, **Lara
Ricote**, **Pierre Novellie**, **Rhys James** and **Those People**.

Those lists are not award shortlists, but they are useful weather signs. The
final week is when critical enthusiasm, industry attention and audience buzz
start to harden into nominations.

## Today's takeaway

At this stage of the Fringe, the safest schedule is often the least
interesting one. Leave room for one show that sounds impractical, stubborn or
plain ridiculous. That is where Edinburgh is still doing its best work.
