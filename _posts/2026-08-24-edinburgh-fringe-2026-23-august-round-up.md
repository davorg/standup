**Sunday 23 August** closed the festival's third weekend with two useful
late-run signals: a national review for one of our favourite shows, and a
newly announced one-off event that sounds almost too Fringe to exist.

## Reviews of the day

The Guardian reviewed
**[Bebe Cave's *Swoon*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/23/bebe-cave-swoon-review-dazzling-portrait-of-marginalised-muses-and-the-male-gaze)**,
calling attention to the same things that made it stand out during our visit:
historical mischief, a chaotic gallery of characters and a sharp eye for the
women made marginal in stories about great male artists. It is gratifying to
see the show continue to gather notice beyond the rooms where early audiences
found it.

The paper also reviewed
**[Megasquirt - *Fully Loaded*](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/23/megasquirt-fully-loaded-review-underbelly-cowgate-edinburgh)**,
Ashley Blair and Drew Reilly's late-night comedy rave at Underbelly Cowgate.
The show mixes songs, sketches and generational anxiety into something
deliberately loud and messy. Late-night Fringe can sometimes mean loose
padding; this sounds more like a show using the hour and the time slot as
part of its argument.

## Pick'n'mix Fringe

The Guardian's
[same-day quick-fire round-up](https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2026/aug/23/edinburgh-fringe-reviews-pick-n-mix)
ranged well beyond straight stand-up, including **Anna Leong Brophy's *Born
Sexy Yesterday*** and a spread of theatre, politics, physical performance and
comic oddity. These broad round-ups are especially useful late in the month.
By now, the biggest names have been reviewed several times; the better
service is often to point audiences towards shows that have not yet been
flattened into consensus.

## One Joke, sixty comics

British Comedy Guide reported that
[One Joke](https://www.comedy.co.uk/fringe/fyi/1160/one-joke-lineup/) will
bring at least **60 performers** to Pleasance Dome on **Wednesday 26 August**.
The rule is pleasingly brutal: each comic gets one joke, then leaves. Jonathan
Oldfield hosts, with ticket money going to The Welcoming, an Edinburgh charity
supporting refugees.

The announced line-up includes **Aisha Amanduri**, **Chris Cantrill**, **David
O'Doherty**, **Demi Adejuyigbe**, **Elf Lyons**, **Jenny Gorelick**,
**John-Luke Roberts**, **Lara Ricote**, **Olga Koch**, **Phil Ellis** and
**Rosalie Minnitt**, among many others.

It is both a charity bill and a neat experiment in comedy discipline. At a
festival where every performer is fighting for an hour of attention, there is
something elegant about making everyone survive on a single gag.

## Today's takeaway

The final week is now in sight. Reviews are still landing, awards shortlists
are imminent and the calendar is filling with one-off events that reward
people who keep looking past the obvious listings.
